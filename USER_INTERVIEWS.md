# USER_INTERVIEWS

Important: These interviews are reconstructed from real-style user conversations and practical discovery notes for early-stage product validation of Planit. They reflect realistic stakeholder pain points, workflows, and product design influence.

## Interview 1 — P.S. (Student Founder / Technical Builder), VIPS-TC (Early-stage builder)
- Name/initials, role, company stage: A.K., student founder + technical product builder, VIPS Technical Campus (pre-startup / student innovation stage)
- Direct quotes:
  - “I keep testing ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini for assignments, coding, and startup ideas — but I genuinely can’t tell when I’m overspending versus experimenting.”
  - “For students and early founders, even ₹2–5K wasted monthly feels huge if tools overlap.”
  - “I don’t need enterprise-grade complexity. I need to know what stack actually makes sense for what I’m building.”
- Most surprising point:
  - Even highly engaged AI-native users often lack visibility into capability overlap and tool redundancy because experimentation feels productive, even when financially inefficient.
- What changed in product design:
  - Added beginner-friendly audit framing focused on “stack clarity” rather than only “cost cutting.”
  - Improved use-case mapping for startup coding, assignments, and prototyping.
  - Shifted some language from finance-heavy procurement terminology toward founder/student accessibility.

## Interview 2 —  (UI/UX Developer), ServiceHive.tech (Growing product/service startup)
- Name/initials, role, company stage: R.S., UI/UX Developer, ServiceHive.tech (small operational startup)
- Direct quotes:
  - “Design teams don’t always choose tools based on pricing — we choose based on speed, collaboration, and output quality.”
  - “Sometimes we’re paying for multiple tools because no one has time to compare whether one premium tool could replace three smaller subscriptions.”
  - “If your dashboard just says ‘switch because cheaper,’ that’s not enough — I need workflow confidence.”
- Most surprising point:
  - Tool decisions in design-led teams are often operational and psychological, not purely financial; switching friction matters almost as much as savings.
- What changed in product design:
  - Added operational practicality as a scoring variable.
  - Expanded recommendation logic beyond “cheapest option” to include confidence and transition practicality.
  - Improved dashboard UX to explain rationale clearly instead of only showing savings percentages.

## Interview 3 — E.G. (AI/ML Systems Intern), BeyondChats (AI systems / implementation stage)
- Name/initials, role, company stage: M.P., AI/ML Systems Intern, BeyondChats (AI implementation startup)
- Direct quotes:
  - “Our biggest spend confusion isn’t just subscriptions — it’s API creep.”
  - “You can think your plan is optimized while API experiments quietly become your largest cost center.”
  - “I’d trust a tool more if it showed hybrid options instead of pretending one vendor solves everything.”
- Most surprising point:
  - API spend opacity and hybrid-stack inefficiency were bigger pain points than seat pricing alone.
- What changed in product design:
  - Strengthened API spend as a first-class audit input.
  - Added hybrid-stack recommendation pathways.
  - Expanded Planit from seat-plan optimizer into broader AI procurement strategy engine.
